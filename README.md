# Post-COVID-19: Deep Image Processing AI to Analyze Social Distancing in a Human Community
## full paper => https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-5559-3_6

In the case of the fight against COVID-19, social distancing has proven to be an effective intervention to minimize disease transmission. AI/Deep Learning has recently lent itself as a great tool to solving almost every conceivable problem in daily life and has shown significant promising results for such problems. In this article, we will explore in-depth how Python, Computer Vision, and Deep Learning can be used to track social distances in public and workplaces. Our study seeks to contribute to the effort in the fight against the pandemic by providing a tool that basically functions as a social distancing monitoring device. Since social distancing is one of the first-line, non-pharmaceutical interventions adopted to combat the COVID-19 pandemic and with growing evidence supporting its effectiveness, we believe that strict accordance with this policy should be encouraged. With the sole purpose of ensuring effective social distancing in public places, our tool will analyze real-time and/or pre-recorded video feed, detect and compute the pairwise metric distances between individuals to validate whether or not social distancing is maintained.


# Proposed Approach
Our model is able to provide analysis of distancing observation in human communityeither in real time or in pre-recorded video feed with a high degree of precision. The model is made up of two phases. The first phase is the human detection andlocalization, and the second phase involves social distancing detection.
* Human Detection Step: For this phase, we first developed a single class object detector which detects with ahigh precision and accuracy. If there is/are person(s) in a pre-recorded or real-timevideo feed, the detector localizes the object (person) and surrounds it with a boundingbox. To perform this task, we used the framework of the faster R-CNN inception v2framework see link. The evolution of the success of object detection could some what be attributed to the remarkable success of deep CNN in image classification on the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC) 2012. The FasterR-CNN was proposed to reduce the computational burden of theproposals generation. This approach consists of two modules.

* Fig. 1 Workflow for people detection and social distancing classificationa Regional Proposal Network (RPN), a fully convolutional network that generatesobject proposals that are fed into the second module. The second module is the FastR-CNN detector whose purpose is to refine the proposals. The difference betweenFaster R-CNN and Fast R-CNN is that Faster R-CNN features architecture such as VGG and Inceptions for proposal generation instead of the selective search algorithmused by Fast-R-CNN. The CNN features are then passed through the region proposalnetwork, and a sliding window is passed through features with potential boundingboxes and scores as the output. Moreover, the Faster R-CNN is faster than the FastR-CNN as it saves computation by computing the features only once. As shown in Fig. 1, this section addresses the necessary steps for establishing awork flow for tracking social distancing on video/image frames. Prepare the images or video feed from a camera which contains people. Applying the faster R-CNN object detector to detect people in images or videofeed. Check the number of persons that are in the images or video feed. Compute the distance between the centroid of the bounding boxes which areenclosed to the detected people.

* Finally, based on the number of people and the measured distance between thecentroid of the bounding boxes, the algorithm will determine whether socialdistancing protocol is being observed or not.4 Faster R-CNN DesignFaster R-CNN architecture is a simple convolution layer with kernel size 3 × 3 anda regional proposal network (RPN), followed by a detector network. First of all, theinput image is redesigned to be 600 px on its shortest side, not exceeding 1000 pxon the long side and passed over RPN. When we pass through the RPN, we have afeature map with a much smaller size from the input. If the input image has 600 ×800 × 3 dimensions, the output feature map would be 37 × 50 × 256 dimensions.Each point in 37 50 is considered as an anchor.RPN is then connected to a Conv layer that includes 3 × 3 filters, 1 padding and512 output channels. The output is connected to two 1 × 1 convolution layers forclassification and box regression, respectively. Each anchor has 3 × 3 boxes in theoriginal image, meaning that the image contains 37 × 50 × 9 boxes. At the sametime, non-max suppression is applied to ensure that the proposed regions do notoverlap.
